No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we touch a tool, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and tonnage of your system, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get an honest recommendation and the complete price up front, whether the job is swapping a worn capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

The problem with HVAC failures is that many of them develop where you can't see them. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down a little at a time, and a weak capacitor lasts right up until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. If your bills are climbing, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many Summerville homeowners call us to evaluate an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, replacing on your own schedule beats a rushed swap in the middle of a heat wave. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Preventive Care

Tune-Ups That Keep Your System Running

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep your home comfortable year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, keeps coils and burners clean, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Summerville HVAC, every plan is built around your home's specific equipment, with scheduled visits that cover coil cleaning, refrigerant charge verification, capacitor and contactor testing, burner and heat exchanger inspection, condensate drain clearing, filter replacement, and a static pressure check on the ductwork.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with spring cooling tune-ups and fall heating tune-ups for most systems. This is especially important in dusty areas, where dirty condenser coils choke heat rejection and drive up energy use. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your equipment's age, your household's comfort needs, and your local climate.

The savings show up quickly. Clean coils move heat with less effort, a correct refrigerant charge takes strain off the compressor, and a fresh filter spares the blower motor. Next to the cost of replacing a system even one year early, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Right-Sized Systems

The Right Size System for Your Home

When it comes to HVAC, bigger is not better. An oversized unit turns on and off too often, wastes energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs nonstop and still falls behind on the hottest afternoons. We don't simply copy the tonnage of the old unit. Instead, we run a proper load calculation that factors in your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

Choosing new equipment is about more than a SEER2 number. We'll compare furnace-and-AC setups with heat pumps, and ducted systems with ductless mini-splits, laying out operating costs, electrical and venting requirements, and the installation itself. Families with a growing household often do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds more even temperatures from room to room and uses less energy. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Common HVAC Problems We Fix in Summerville

Homes in Summerville run into a familiar set of heating and cooling failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Summerville HVAC handles them all: no cooling, air that blows but never gets cold, furnaces that won't ignite or short-cycle, frozen evaporator coils, refrigerant leaks, condensers that hum without starting, thermostats that lose their settings, clogged condensate drains, and mini-splits flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The right repair depends entirely on the part that failed. On cooling systems, that usually means a capacitor, contactor, or fan motor. On furnaces, it tends to be an igniter, flame sensor, or control board. Leaky ductwork bleeding conditioned air into the attic is sealed and repaired, and we recover and recharge the refrigerant once the leak itself is repaired. Some of these issues are just inconvenient, but a cracked heat exchanger or a clogged condensate drain can be a real safety or water-damage risk, so we treat it with urgency.
