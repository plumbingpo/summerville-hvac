From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower moving heat through the ducts. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In Summerville homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, with a meter at every step. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. Safety checks are never optional on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard is not visible at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower are matched the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are stocked on every truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. Signs You Need Furnace Repair in Summerville

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late

Why Professional Furnace Repair Matters

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue all share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower circulates. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Swapping the switch in that second case removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

At Summerville HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, keeps causing trouble. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to flag airflow problems early, and leak-test every gas connection with instruments, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, you will get an honest assessment.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Change the filter monthly during heating season; it is the cheapest furnace part you will ever buy
  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
  • Notice how the furnace sounds at startup, because a quiet light-off is healthy and a boom is not

Should You DIY or Call a Pro?

A homeowner can swap a filter with ease, but diagnosing capacitors, refrigerant charge, control boards, and error codes calls for the equipment and know-how only a pro brings. Consider the following comparison:
